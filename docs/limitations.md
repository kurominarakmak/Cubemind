# Limitations

- **Reconstruction selection bias.** The CFOP action model uses 9,109 replay-valid solves from 9,288 CFOP-filtered entries, split across 386 solver identities. A separate all-method 3×3 crawl found 10,474 entries and 10,274 valid reconstructions, but it did not return identical CFOP IDs (120 broad-scan-only and 100 focused-scan-only). The roughly 14,000 site-wide count also includes other events; OH and non-3×3 events are excluded here. The CFOP filtered crawl had 179 rejected entries and the broader crawl had 200.
- **Reconstruction labels show actions, not intent.** The model cannot infer what a solver saw during inspection, why they selected a Cross color, or whether a move was intended as pseudo-slotting unless the reconstruction annotates it. A recorded selected color does not reveal the solver's white/yellow/all-six capability.
- **Next-action imitation is not a solver.** The human model predicts one demonstrated action from state and context. Its 64.92% held-out F2L Top-1 was higher than the new hierarchical model's 58.30%, but using it in the old flat search completed F2L on 0/100. It remains an action predictor, not an end-to-end route planner.
- **Full-cube reliability is not perfect.** The earlier flat policy/value solver still achieved 0/100 full solves. The Milestone C/D integrated EO-aware route now solves 90/100 fresh scrambles (Wilson 95% interval 82.56–94.48%) and meets the 90% point target, but ten cases failed. The deterministic reference solved 100/100. The separate hierarchical Cross+F2L controller's 98/100 result remains a historical milestone on a different seed.
- **Human strategy labels are partial.** The hierarchical model learns pair order from simulator-verified single-pair transitions and can include setup moves in a pair segment. Simultaneous pair completions, explicit pseudo-slot intent, inspection decisions, and multi-pair intent are not modeled as separate labels.
- **Small diagnostic samples.** Cross-source checks and the value-head ablation used 9–10 development cases; curriculum-stage checks used five held-out starts per stage. The final 100-case end-to-end benchmark is stronger evidence for full F2L completion, but it does not establish robustness across all scramble distributions or cuber styles.
- **Move count and compute trade-off.** Successful hierarchical solves averaged 34.72 STM, about 4.80 more turns than the deterministic reference on the same cases. Search averaged 8.83 seconds versus 1.56 seconds for the package baseline. The matched uniform-action search completed 22/100 compared with 98/100 for neural action guidance; its value head and pair selector remain active, so the ablation does not isolate those components.
- **Human F2L context is simplified at inference.** The earlier reco.nz flat hybrid fixes its strategy context to standard F2L and Cross to white on Down. The new hierarchical model normalizes Cross color and chooses pair order, but it still lacks explicit inspection and pair-strategy annotations when the source does not record them.
- **Uneven phase annotations.** The current corpus has 39,164 Cross, 30,619 Cross+1, 253,051 F2L, and 1,263 explicitly annotated ZBLS actions. ZBLS is a small minority; its test accuracy should be interpreted with its phase sample count. Phase scores remain sensitive to source annotation coverage.
- **Synthetic ZBLL distribution.** The classifier trains and tests on simulator-generated algorithm cases. The pose split holds out AUF/orientation presentations while retaining every case ID in all splits. Exact recognition and the classifier both score 100% on 200 final test presentations, but this does not establish unseen-case generalization or human recognition. Neural ZBLL solving reached 194/200 on controlled eligible states but only 7/10 on eligible states produced by the full F2L pipeline; the latter sample is small and indicates distribution shift.
- **Algorithm table scope.** The database has 240 OLL variants, 104 imported PLL variants plus 21 PLL primaries, and one primary algorithm per ZBLL/ZBLS case. The ZBLS importer selects one orientation bucket and omits alternatives; each included sequence is simulator-verified. The optional preference ranker covers only human-matched OLL/PLL variants, not ZBLL/ZBLS, execution ergonomics, or recognition difficulty.
- **External full-CFOP baseline.** The policy comparison uses a deterministic CFOP package and its bundled algorithm sets. It is separate from CubeMind's Alg-Trainer ZBLL and CubeRoot ZBLS files. It is not a human policy or a neural model.
- **Color neutrality.** The paired solver benchmark fixes white on D to isolate the policy change. It does not compare white-only, yellow-only, dual-color, or full-color-neutral solvers. Human rows preserve selected Cross color but set solver-wide capability to unknown.
- **Cross model distinction.** The original Cross MLP/Transformer are trained from a synthetic shortest-Cross oracle. They remain useful pipeline baselines but must not be presented as human imitation.
- **Timing.** Reported times are computational measurements on the local machine and solver package. Human recognition, turning, and execution times are not measured.
- **Limited repeatability evidence.** The hierarchical checkpoint was selected from two training seeds, and the 100-scramble benchmark reports Wilson intervals, but the benchmark was not repeated across training seeds or scramble seeds. The human-source and value-head diagnostics are small one-run samples; broad claims need repeated runs.
- **Expert-aware F2L evidence is preliminary.** The fresh 100-scramble run reached 95/100 complete F2L for the expert-aware mode (Wilson 95% interval 88.82–97.85%), versus 90/100 for both the exact original and legal-normalized planner on the same scrambles. The paired five-case advantage was not statistically significant (exact p=0.227). The preserved historical 98/100 result used a different scramble seed. Expert next-action and pair-order Top-1 improved on solver-held-out reconstructions, but those are teacher-forced agreement measures rather than blind human ratings. The selected action weight is 1.0, so planner move probabilities contribute no weight; the planner's value and verified subgoal search remain active. On shared successful scrambles the new route averaged 0.49 more STM than the exact original planner. Component search ablations used only 20 scrambles, and execution-cost search optimizes STM rather than measured ergonomics or speed.
- **EO-aware routing has not shown a full-solve gain.** Only 10/100 expert-aware F2L endpoints were ZBLL-eligible. The neural OLL→PLL full route solved 93/100, versus 90/100 for EO-aware routing; the paired difference was not significant (exact p=0.25). F2L-to-ZBLL planning and larger eligible-state samples remain future work.
- **V2 ZBLS search is not reliable yet.** On 36 case-disjoint held-out cases, synthetic-guided search solved 5/36 (Wilson 95% interval 6.1–28.7%); human-only and mixed search each solved 2/36, greedy solved 1/36, and matched uniform search solved 0/36. In the full pipeline, ZBLS completed 8/98 attempts (8.2%) and conventional F2L was ZBLL-eligible on 16/95 completed endpoints. These results do not meet a practical ZBLS solve-rate target.
- **V2 human ZBLS evidence is sparse.** The human-only and mixed evaluations contain 97 held-out actions from 9 explicitly annotated trajectories. The mixed model reached 37.11% Top-1 on those rows; this is action agreement, not evidence of pro-level ZBLS or human-like decisions.
- **Global planning is bounded route selection.** V2 compares conventional neural OLL→PLL, EO-aware neural last layer, and one three-pair-prefix neural-ZBLS branch from one Cross and F2L search. It selects minimum STM among verified candidates, then rotations and nodes. It does not enumerate multiple Cross/F2L candidates, optimize expert-sequence likelihood across complete solutions, use a learned downstream value model, or prove global optimality.
- **The 100-case V2 planner result is preliminary.** On seed 20261128 it solved 95/100 (Wilson 95% interval 88.82–97.85%), versus 94/100 for each fixed neural route and 100/100 for the deterministic reference. The planner's one-case gain over conventional neural CFOP was not significant (exact McNemar p=1.0). On common successful cases it selected 0.70 fewer STM on average by design; this is a move-count optimization, not evidence of expert-like choices. It used 9.55 seconds and 16,515 nodes per scramble on average because it compares multiple neural searches. The deterministic reference averaged 1.82 seconds and 55.03 STM. This is one scramble seed and one checkpoint set.
- **Expert Cross imitation does not learn inspection intent.** V2.1 adds a separate human-only Cross action policy and held-out X-Cross/Cross+1 annotation counts, but it does not predict Cross color, X-Cross actions, pseudo-slot intent, or a cuber's color-neutral capability. Its 47.37% held-out Top-1 is action agreement only. Six-frame evaluation is a software capability, not evidence of human six-color inspection.
- **Algorithm preference evidence is limited.** The random-initialized OLL/PLL ranker uses 2,169 solver-disjoint human choices after requiring the exact matched table candidate to solve its input state in the simulator. Held-out Top-1 was 72.19%, above shortest (36.69%) but below most-frequent training choice (75.15%; paired exact p=0.227). On 100 paired full solves, preference ranking and shortest both solved 97/100; preference used 0.87 more STM and 0.28 s more software time on average. The model has not established an advantage over frequency or at end-to-end solving, covers only represented OLL/PLL cases, and says nothing about human recognition or execution time. ZBLL/ZBLS preferences remain unsupported.
- **ZBLS recognition is replay-based.** All 302 stored setup/algorithm pairs verify, but runtime scans candidates against the simulator goal instead of recognizing a canonical ZBLS case identity. The imported CubeRoot snapshot records no reuse license; redistribution remains uncleared.
- **Global planning is still bounded and prefix-limited.** The planner compares six Cross frames and CFOP/ZBLS routes from one selected Cross/F2L search. It does not retain a beam of Cross solutions, pair orders and insertions, learn downstream value, or prove global optimality.
- **No V2 recovery training.** The new ZBLS policy has not yet been trained on on-policy failure/recovery states from the integrated V2 pipeline. The low pipeline success rate and the controlled case search failure rate make this the main next model-data gap.
- **V2 checkpoints are local research artifacts.** The human-only and mixed weights were trained using explicit local reco.nz annotations. Their presence in the local workspace does not grant redistribution rights; they have not been uploaded or published.
