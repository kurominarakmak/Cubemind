# Portfolio summary

## V2.1 status

V2.1 adds 57 OLL identities/240 variants, 21 PLL identities with 104 replay-matched SpeedCubeDB variants, exact 493-case ZBLL/PLL recognition, and 302 simulator-verified ZBLS setup/algorithm pairs. A 118,418-parameter expert Cross policy was trained from scratch on solver-disjoint human Cross labels: 47.37% Top-1 and 71.67% Top-3 on 3,996 held-out actions, compared with 12.09%/27.08% majority baselines. It is action imitation, not evidence of expert inspection decisions.

The V2.1 fresh 100-scramble paired run solved 93/100 with neural Last-Layer/ZBLS generation, 97/100 with verified symbolic retrieval plus bounded ZBLS prefix search, and 100/100 with the deterministic reference. Conventional neural OLL→PLL solved 93/100, symbolic OLL/PLL 94/100, and each EO-aware CFOP route 93/100 or 94/100 respectively. ZBLL eligibility after completed F2L was 14/100. The optional 8,033-parameter human OLL/PLL preference ranker reached 72.19% held-out Top-1 on 169 choices, below the most-frequent baseline (75.15%) but above shortest (36.69%). Its statistical comparison against frequency was not significant. On the same 100 scrambles, learned preference and shortest ranking both solved 97/100; preference ranking averaged 0.87 STM and 0.28 s more. It therefore did not improve end-to-end results. No model or dataset was uploaded or published.

CubeMind now spans three deliberately separate research tracks:

- **Cross foundation:** exact shortest-Cross labels, from-scratch MLP/Transformer baselines, held-out scramble evaluation, and policy-guided search.
- **Human solve corpus:** a broad 3×3 all-method scan with 10,474 unique index entries and 10,274 replay-valid reconstructions, stored separately from the **CFOP action model**. The model uses 9,109 replay-valid CFOP solves, 324,097 Cross/Cross+1/F2L/ZBLS action rows, and solver-held-out splits. The final test set contains 38,057 actions from 1,048 solves and 39 held-out solvers.
- **ZB last layer:** replay-verified 493-case ZBLL/PLL table from Alg-Trainer and 302-case ZBLS table imported from CubeRoot's public API, exact AUF/orientation-normalized case recognition, a from-scratch neural classifier, and paired conventional/EO-aware CFOP solver benchmarking.
- **End-to-end solver study:** a from-scratch policy/value MLP, autoregressive greedy inference, neural-guided beam search, synthetic DAgger relabeling, a separate reco.nz-F2L hybrid, and explicit simulator-verified failure reporting. On the latest 100 held-out scrambles, both synthetic and human-F2L search completed Cross on 99/100 but F2L on 0/100, so full solves were 0/100; the deterministic CFOP comparison solved 100/100. See `docs/experiment_report.md` for settings, phase rates, timing, and confidence interval.
- **Milestone B hierarchical F2L:** a separate random-initialized action/value/pair-selection model with pair-conditioned search. On 100 new scrambles it preserved Cross and simulator-verified all four F2L pairs on 98/100, compared with 22/100 for a matched uniform-action ablation, 0/100 greedy, and 0/100 flat F2L baselines. It stops before the Last Layer and averages 4.80 more STM than the deterministic reference on shared successful cases. See the Milestone B section of `docs/experiment_report.md`.
- **Expert-aware F2L extension:** new canonical action and pair-order networks trained from scratch on solver-disjoint reco.nz demonstrations, without replacing the historical planner. Held-out action Top-1 was 69.13% versus 60.57% for the planner; pair-order Top-1 was 62.34% versus 56.89%. Validation chose the expert-only action endpoint. On 100 fresh scrambles it completed F2L on 95/100, while the exact original and legal-normalized planner each completed 90/100; the paired gain was not significant (p=0.227). It averaged 34.40 STM versus 34.07 for the original planner. In a separate 20-case ablation, the combined expert policy completed 20/20, action-only 19/20, pair-order-only 18/20, uniform action 9/20, and STM-cost search 20/20 at higher compute cost. These small results are exploratory. The historical 98/100 baseline remains intact on its separate seed. See `docs/experiment_report.md` and `artifacts/expert-aware-f2l-benchmark.json`.

- **Milestones C and D Last Layer/full cube:** the from-scratch expert-aware neural policy achieved 158/158 PLL, 158/158 OLL→PLL, and 194/200 (97%) eligible ZBLL completions on held-out controlled states. Exact recognition and its neural classifier both scored 100% on 200 known-case presentations; the classifier does not choose solving moves. On 100 fresh full-cube scrambles, the expert-aware neural OLL→PLL route solved 93, the EO-aware neural route solved 90 (Wilson 95% interval 82.56–94.48%), and greedy solved 10. A value-only ablation solved 0; the deterministic reference solved 100. The EO-aware point rate meets the 90% target, but only 10/100 states were ZBLL-eligible after F2L and direct ZBLL solved 7/10. EO-aware routing did not significantly improve completion over conventional neural OLL→PLL (paired exact p=0.25). Every successful route was independently replay-verified; human recognition and execution time remain unmeasured. See `docs/experiment_report.md` and `artifacts/last-layer-benchmark.json`.

In the 20-scramble solver comparison, conventional CFOP solved all 20, with ZBLL eligibility after F2L on 1/20 (5%). The EO-aware ZBLS→ZBLL policy solved all 20 and reached eligibility on 20/20. Mean solver move count was 51.50 STM for conventional CFOP and 47.15 STM for EO-aware CFOP. Mean compute time was 1,482.7 ms and 711.4 ms respectively. These are outcomes of a small deterministic solver benchmark, not human times or expert policy quality.

An earlier synthetic pose-generalization evaluation scored 99.23% Top-1 for the neural ZBLL classifier and 100% for exact recognition. The later 200-presentation test scored 100% for both. In either case, the exact lookup remains the baseline; classification is separate from move generation and does not improve on deterministic recognition.

The human next-action model and synthetic ZBLL case classifier answer different questions: one imitates reconstructed moves, while the other classifies known cases from generated states. The earlier flat solver's 0/100 F2L result and the hierarchical controller's 98/100 Cross+F2L result remain separate historical experiments; Milestone C/D now adds the Last Layer and full-cube route summarized above. Larger solver-held-out human samples, repeated training seeds, timed human recognition, and broad cross-neutrality comparisons remain future work.

On the final reco.nz test split, the context model scored 56.75% Top-1, 82.15% Top-3, and 90.60% Top-5. It scored 61.42% on ordinary F2L and 58.67% on 150 annotated ZBLS actions; the state-only model scored 49.76% Top-1 and the training-frequency baseline 18.12%. Earlier runs used different test partitions, so their scores are not direct paired comparisons.
